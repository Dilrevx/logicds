The repair captures the key and IV lengths just once before invoking the cipher’s initialization function. In the original code, the key and IV lengths were recalculated inline when passed to the encryption initialization function. This introduces potential problems if the OSSL_PARAM array (provided later) or other operations within EVP_CipherInit_ex2() manipulate the context. If the key or IV values are being re-read after any modifications, it could lead to truncation or buffer overruns, which in turn may cause unintended behavior like a truncated IV. A truncated IV may lead to non-uniqueness, ultimately compromising the confidentiality of the cipher mode.

Here’s how the repair works:

1. Before calling the encryption initialization function (ctx->cipher->einit), the repair computes the lengths of the key and IV just once and assigns them to local variables (key_len and iv_len). This ensures that regardless of any side effects caused by processing additional parameters (params), the lengths remain consistent.

2. When the eit function is called, these pre-computed lengths (key_len and iv_len) are passed in as arguments. This method prevents potential issues like truncation or overrun because the lengths are locked in at this point. They won’t be recalculated or affected by any parameter processing that happens later in EVP_CipherInit_ex2().

3. By doing so, the fix ensures that the cipher initialization process relies on a consistent interpretation of the key and IV lengths. This consistency minimizes the risk of incorrectly treating the key or IV, thus averting vulnerabilities associated with their mismanagement.

The change does not alter the core functionality of the cipher initialization — it simply adds a step to capture these lengths explicitly and store them. This additional safeguard directly addresses the described vulnerability and reinforces the algorithm’s security by ensuring that any parameter processing that follows won’t inadvertently lead to truncation or misuse of the IV (or key).

In summary, the repair fixes the vulnerability by:
• Reading the key and IV lengths once and storing them in variables.
• Using these stored values in the subsequent encryption initialization function.
• Preventing any later modifications (via parameter processing or other side effects) from causing an unintended change in these critical lengths.

This approach ensures the integrity and security of the symmetric cipher initialization, protecting the algorithm from potential vulnerabilities related to key and IV mismanagement.
