The repair removes the insecure downgrade that was introduced by the problematic logic. In the original code, if the OCSP no-check extension was present (indicated by cert->ocspNoCheckSet) and the verification mode was set for VERIFY_OCSP, the verification was blindly downgraded to NO_VERIFY. This allowed responders to potentially bypass critical certificate checks simply by including a flag in their certificate, opening the door to an attacker substituting an illegitimate responder certificate with one improperly flagged to bypass checks.

By removing the conditional downgrade, the repair stops automatically disabling OCSP verification based solely on the presence of the no-check flag. This change ensures that the OCSP responder’s certificate is still subject to proper verification processes, maintaining the intended security guarantees. The new code includes comments explaining why the behavior was removed, thereby enhancing maintainability and clarity for future reviews or audits.

Thus, the vulnerability is fixed by not allowing a potentially compromised or misconfigured OCSP certificate to bypass the critical validation steps. This prevents an attacker from exploiting the certificate no-check flag as a method to undermine the security of OCSP verification.
