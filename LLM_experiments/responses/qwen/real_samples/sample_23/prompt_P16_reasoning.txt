### Understanding the Problem

In cryptographic operations, an Initialization Vector (IV) is a block of bits that is used by several modes of block ciphers to randomize the encryption and make each message unique, even if the same key is used multiple times. For AES-CBC and DES3 encryption modes, the IV plays a pivotal role in ensuring that identical plaintext blocks encrypt to different ciphertext blocks. If the IV is not chosen randomly and uniquely for each encryption operation, it can lead to severe security vulnerabilities, including the possibility of attackers predicting the IV and thereby decrypting messages.

Since the IV is meant to be unpredictable and unique for each encryption operation, setting it to zero makes it predictable, which is a significant security flaw.
