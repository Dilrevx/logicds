One of the fundamental security features of DTLS, as with TLS, is the use of random values during the handshake process. These random values are crucial for ensuring that each handshake is unique and unpredictable, which helps prevent various attacks, including replay attacks and man-in-the-middle attacks.

### Importance of Cryptographic Randomness

The use of cryptographic randomness in secure protocols like DTLS is non-negotiable. Fixed or predictable values can be exploited by attackers to:
- **Replay Attacks**: Replay previously recorded handshake messages to impersonate legitimate parties.
- **Man-in-the-Middle Attacks**: Intercept and modify communication by predicting the expected random values.
- **Brute Force Attacks**: Reduce the complexity of brute force attacks on encrypted data by knowing or predicting parts of the encryption keys derived from the handshake.

By ensuring that each handshake uses a unique, unpredictable random value, the repair enhances the security of the DTLS implementation, making it resilient against these types of attacks and adhering to the security principles of modern cryptographic protocols.
