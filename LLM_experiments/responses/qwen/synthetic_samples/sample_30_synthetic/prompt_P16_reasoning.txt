This approach is flawed because it fails to consider the `issuer` of the certificate, which is a critical component in determining the validity of the certificate according to its corresponding CRL. The `subject` refers to the entity to which the certificate was issued, while the `issuer` is the entity that issued the certificate. Both are necessary for accurate CRL verification.

When the `subject` is used to fetch CRLs, the system mistakenly checks the same field twice instead of verifying both the `subject` and `issuer` fields. This discrepancy can lead to a situation where a revoked certificate might bypass the revocation check, posing a significant security risk. Specifically, an attacker could potentially present a certificate that appears valid because the system does not correctly cross-reference the certificate against the proper CRL based on the `issuer`.

### The Importance of Issuer Verification

The `issuer` field is essential in CRL verification because it specifies the Certificate Authority (CA) that issued the certificate. Each CA maintains its own set of CRLs that list certificates it has revoked. Therefore, to accurately determine if a certificate has been revoked, the system must fetch the CRLs associated with the correct CA, identified by the `issuer` field.
