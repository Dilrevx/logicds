### Understanding the Vulnerability

TLS, or Transport Layer Security, is a cryptographic protocol designed to provide secure communications over a computer network. A fundamental part of the TLS handshake is the ChangeCipherSpec (CCS) message, which signals the transition from plaintext to encrypted communication using the negotiated keys. Typically, the CCS message should be processed at a specific point in the handshake sequence to ensure that encryption starts only after both parties have exchanged their respective Finished messages, confirming the successful establishment of shared secrets.

However, in the original buggy code snippet, there was no explicit mechanism to ensure that the CCS message had been received and processed correctly before transitioning to the `SSL_ST_OK` state. This oversight allowed an attacker to potentially inject a CCS message prematurely, which could lead to the misuse of a zero-length master key or other security compromises.
