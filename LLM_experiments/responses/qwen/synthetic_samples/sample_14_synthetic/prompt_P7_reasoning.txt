### Understanding the Vulnerability

The original code snippet does not explicitly check for the presence and validity of the CCS message before proceeding with the `Finished` state of the handshake. In the context of the SSL/TLS protocol, the CCS message signals the transition from using unencrypted to encrypted communication. It is crucial that this message is received and processed correctly to ensure that both parties in the communication agree on the encryption parameters and keys.

### The Role of CCS in the Handshake Process

During a typical SSL/TLS handshake, the CCS message is sent by the client (or server, depending on the direction of the handshake) immediately after the `ChangeCipherSpec` alert is sent. This alert indicates that the party is about to start using the newly negotiated cipher suite and keys for encrypting its messages. The `Finished` message, which follows, is encrypted using the new keys and serves as proof that the handshake was successful and that both parties have agreed on the same encryption parameters.
