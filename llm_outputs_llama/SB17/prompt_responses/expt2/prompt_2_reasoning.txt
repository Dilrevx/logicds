The reasoning behind this change is based on the way CRLs are structured. A CRL is a list of certificates that have been revoked by the issuing Certificate Authority (CA). Each entry in the CRL contains information about the revoked certificate, including the subject and issuer fields. The subject field identifies the entity to which the certificate was issued, while the issuer field identifies the CA that issued the certificate.

When verifying a client's certificate, it is essential to check both the subject and issuer fields of the CRL entry. The subject field is used to identify the client's certificate, while the issuer field is used to identify the CA that issued the certificate. By checking both fields, the function can ensure that the client's certificate has not been revoked by the issuing CA.

In the original code, the `get1_crls` function is called with the `subject` as an argument, which means that the function is only checking the subject field of the CRL entry. This is insufficient, as it does not take into account the issuer field. By changing the argument to `issuer`, the function will now correctly check the issuer field of the CRL entry, ensuring that the client's certificate has not been revoked by the issuing CA.
