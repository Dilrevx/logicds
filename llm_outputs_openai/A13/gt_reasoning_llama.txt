The vulnerability in question is related to the ineffective zone transfer controls for writable DLZ (Dynamically Loaded Zones) zones. The issue arises from the fact that the `allowzonexfr` method is not being called for such zones. This method is crucial in determining whether a zone transfer is allowed or not.

In the buggy code, the `allowzonexfr` method is only called when the normal zone table does not have a match and the zone is not marked as a DLZ zone in the zone table. However, this approach is flawed because it does not account for writable DLZ zones that are not marked as such in the zone table.

The repair addresses this issue by modifying the condition to call the `allowzonexfr` method. Specifically, it checks if the result is not successful or if the zone type is a DLZ zone. This ensures that the `allowzonexfr` method is called for all DLZ zones, including writable ones, regardless of whether they are marked as such in the zone table.

By making this change, the repair ensures that zone transfer controls are effective for all DLZ zones, including writable ones. This prevents unauthorized zone transfers and enhances the security of the system. The `allowzonexfr` method can now correctly determine whether a zone transfer is allowed or not, and the system can respond accordingly.

In essence, the repair fixes the vulnerability by ensuring that the `allowzonexfr` method is called for all DLZ zones, thereby enforcing zone transfer controls and preventing potential security breaches.
