By moving the validity and hostname checks to the top of the routine, any problems with the leaf certificate can be detected right away. If the certificate validity check fails, the code checks whether the error is associated with the expected condition for a leaf certificate (typically an “unable to get issuer certificate” error). If the error differs from this expected scenario, it is immediately recorded in the error stack at the depth corresponding to the leaf certificate (depth zero in this context). This early capture prevents the error from being lost if the process continues due to a callback’s instruction, a scenario that could otherwise mask the true reason behind a failure.

Similarly, the hostname verification is performed right after the certificate validity check. Should this check fail, any resulting error from the hostname verification is promptly stored in the error stack at the corresponding depth. This approach guarantees that both types of errors—those related to the certificate’s trustworthiness and those related to its matching hostname—are preserved and accessible for later stages of error handling or logging.

Once the leaf certificate has passed its validity and hostname checks, the repair then moves on to duplicate and store the certificate chain. This safeguard prevents further operations on a potentially corrupted or incomplete chain state.

However, to maintain a complete log of errors across the verification depth, the error stack is updated with this reset error state for the current depth. This design pattern ensures that any valid chain has its corresponding error entry initialized in the error stack, even if previous errors were encountered during the verification process.
