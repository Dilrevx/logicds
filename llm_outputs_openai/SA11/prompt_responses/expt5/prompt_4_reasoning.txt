The repair streamlines the certificate revocation logic by removing the dependency on a time-based check, which was the source of the vulnerability. Originally, after validating that the certificate's serial number matched an entry in the certificate revocation list (CRL), the code would also check whether the certificate's revocation date had passed relative to the local system time. This secondary check was intended to confirm that the revocation was current. However, because it relied on the system’s clock, an attacker could manipulate the clock to cheat the system, allowing a revoked certificate to be wrongly accepted.

In the repaired code, the logic is simplified: once the certificate serial number match is confirmed (by ensuring that both the lengths and the actual bytes of the serial numbers are identical), the certificate is immediately flagged as revoked by returning 1. By removing the following "if (Helper.mbedtls_x509_time_is_past(cur.revocationDate))" check, the repair eliminates any reliance on evaluating the certificate's revocation date, which could vary based on potentially compromised time settings.

This change is a crucial improvement because:
1. It removes the attacker-controlled factor (i.e., the system clock) entirely from the decision process. Previously, the check depended on the local time, which an attacker could easily alter to make a revoked certificate appear valid.
2. It simplifies the revocation check, making it dependent solely on the certificate’s unique identifier (its serial number), which is immutable and defined by the issuing authority.
3. By immediately marking any certificate that appears in the CRL as revoked, regardless of the revocation date, the system avoids any ambiguity that might arise from time computations or errors related to incorrect system time.

In summary, the repair ensures that the decision-making process for certificate revocation is based exclusively on a definitive match in the certificate identifiers, thereby removing the risk associated with local clock manipulation. The elimination of the revocation date verification step guarantees that any certificate listed in the CRL is unequivocally treated as revoked, strengthening the system against attacks that could exploit time-dependent logic vulnerabilities.
