The repair makes two crucial adjustments to ensure that the certificate chain and its associated untrusted counter remain consistent and accurate:

1. Removing Manual Counter Decrementing Inside the Loop:
   Originally, when an alternative valid chain was found, the code entered a loop that not only removed certificates from the chain but also manually decremented the untrusted certificate counter (ctx->last_untrusted) with each pop. This manual decrementing risked misalignment between the actual number of certificates remaining in the chain and the value held in ctx->last_untrusted, potentially leading to an incorrect evaluation of trust later on. By removing the decrement operation within the loop, the repair eliminates the possibility of the counter being updated inaccurately with each certificate removal.

2. Recalculating the Untrusted Certificate Counter After Pruning:
   After the loop has pruned undesired certificates from the chain, the repair recalculates the correct number of remaining untrusted certificates by using sk_X509_num(ctx->chain) and assigns that value directly to ctx->last_untrusted. This approach ensures that the counter truly reflects the current state of the certificate chain—no more, no less—regardless of how many certificates were removed during the pruning. By doing so, it prevents a scenario where a certificate chain could be misrepresented (for example, a valid leaf certificate mistakenly viewed as if it belongs to a trusted Certification Authority) due to an incorrect counter value.

By synchronizing the count with the actual state of the certificate chain, the repair effectively closes the vulnerability. The original issue stemmed from the potential that a valid leaf certificate, when processed through an alternative chain, could have its associated counter mismanaged—leading to certificate verifications being performed under incorrect assumptions of trust. With the updated process, since the counter accurately reflects only the certificates that remain, any subsequent verification steps use the correct data.

In summary, the repair achieves the following:
• It eliminates the risk of miscounting untrusted certificates during the chain manipulation by removing in-loop manual decrements.
• It recalculates and resets the untrusted count immediately after pruning, ensuring consistency and correctness.
• This accurate bookkeeping prevents an attacker from exploiting the processing of alternative chains to spoof a Certification Authority role.

Thus, the chain’s integrity is maintained, and the vulnerability—where a leaf certificate could erroneously trigger certificate verifications under a false premise—is effectively mitigated.
