The repair addresses the vulnerability by changing how the certificate chain and its associated counter (ctx->last_untrusted) are updated when an alternative chain is detected.

In the original code, when a valid alternative chain was found, the loop removed certificates from the chain one by one while manually decrementing the untrusted certificate counter (ctx->last_untrusted) for each certificate removed. This manual adjustment could lead to inconsistencies between the actual number of certificates in the chain and the value stored in the counter. As a result, later validations might misinterpret the structure of the chain, potentially allowing a leaf certificate to be wrongly treated as coming from a trusted certification authority.

The repair does two key things:

1. It removes the manual decrement of the counter within the loop. Instead of adjusting ctx->last_untrusted on every iteration, the loop simply removes the certificates that are not needed.
2. After the loop has pruned the certificate chain, the repair recalculates the number of certificates in the chain using an API function (sk_X509_num) and then resets ctx->last_untrusted based on the new length of the chain.

By deferring the update of the counter until after all unnecessary certificates are popped, the repair ensures that the counter accurately reflects the current state of the certificate chain. This synchronization prevents errors in subsequent certificate verification decisions where an incorrect count of untrusted certificates might otherwise allow a non-authoritative key (or a leaf certificate) to bypass proper trust evaluation.

Overall, the repair improves the chain’s integrity by ensuring that the count of untrusted certificates is derived directly from the current certificate chain rather than being manually decremented. This adjustment removes the possibility of the certificate chain structure being out of sync with the counter, thus mitigating the risk where a leaf certificate could spoof a Certification Authority role during the verification process.
