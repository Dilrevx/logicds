The original code potentially allowed certificates with the OCSP no-check extension to bypass revocation verification regardless of their role in the certificate hierarchy. In other words, if a certificate had the ocspNoCheck flag set and the verification mode was for OCSP, the code would simply disable the check. This has dangerous implications especially if a certificate is not meant to have this relaxation—such as a CA or intermediate certificate. This could leave an attacker the opportunity to forge or manipulate certificate chains in a way that makes revoked certificates appear valid.

The repair introduces an additional check to ensure that this relaxation is only applied to end-entity certificates. By enforcing that the certificate must be an end-entity certificate (via a method like isEndEntity()), we make sure that the optimization for skipping OCSP validation is only done when it is considered safe by the certificate hierarchy rules. End-entity certificates are generally the final certificates issued to users or services and are less trusted than CA certificates, so the exception is acceptable in that context, while CA certificates should not have their revocation checks bypassed regardless of the OCSP no-check flag.

This additional validation step prevents misuse in scenarios where a CA or intermediate certificate might erroneously or maliciously set the ocspNoCheck flag, which, in the original code, could have allowed a complete bypass of revocation checks in the presence of the insecure flag. The updated logic thereby eliminates a potential attack vector by ensuring the security policy remains intact: only end-entity certificates are given this exemption, while all CA certificates and intermediates are still subject to proper revocation verification.

In summary, the patch works by adding a condition that checks the certificate's role in the chain. This ensures that the relaxation of the OCSP verification is only applied in the proper context (i.e., for end-entity certificates), significantly reducing the risk of unintended or malicious bypassing of revocation checks in critical parts of the certificate chain.
