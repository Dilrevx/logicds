The repair addresses the vulnerability by ensuring that the nonce (IV) length is validated against the correct constant, CHACHA20_POLY1305_MAX_IVLEN, instead of CHACHA_CTR_SIZE.

This means that if an application mistakenly supplies a nonce longer than 12 bytes, it might believe that modifying the unused leading bytes produces a unique nonce; in reality, the encryption only considers the final 12 bytes. This can lead to accidental nonce reuse—a dangerous situation that compromises both the confidentiality and integrity of encrypted data.

By replacing CHACHA_CTR_SIZE with CHACHA20_POLY1305_MAX_IVLEN, the validation check now correctly enforces the maximum acceptable length for nonce values in the context of ChaCha20‑Poly1305. It clearly prevents the possibility of enabling nonces that are longer than the defined maximum (which could lead to misinterpretation where the application might try to generate uniqueness in the unused bytes).

This repair is important because ensuring that each nonce is unique per encryption operation is critical to maintaining the security properties of an AEAD cipher like ChaCha20‑Poly1305. With the updated validation, any input nonce is checked to ensure it does not exceed CHACHA20_POLY1305_MAX_IVLEN, thereby reducing the risk of the application inadvertently encrypting multiple messages with a reused nonce.
