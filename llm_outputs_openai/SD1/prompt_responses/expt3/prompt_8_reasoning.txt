The repair saves the current SRTP transmission and reception policies before stopping the SRTP transport. In the original code, the policies were obtained after stopping SRTP, which risked losing or corrupting essential security configurations. By capturing the policy settings (tx and rx) upfront, the repair ensures that the correct cryptographic parameters are preserved.

After saving the policies, the SRTP transport is stopped. This is necessary to reinitialize secure communication following replay detection errors. Once the transport is stopped, the restart procedure is initiated using the previously stored policy values. This guarantees that the restarted SRTP is configured with the proper security settings.

Finally, the code verifies that the restart was successful and that SRTP remains active by checking the status and the bypass flag. Only then does it proceed to process the incoming packet securely. This reordering of operations eliminates the risk of inadvertently reverting to basic RTP due to lost or misconfigured SRTP settings.

Overall, the repair ensures that the critical SRTP configuration is maintained during a restart, thereby mitigating the vulnerability where insecure media transport could be used if SRTP settings were not correctly re-established.
