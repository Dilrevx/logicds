The repair addresses a subtle but critical flaw in the original OCSP verification logic. In the original code, if the certificate being verified had the NoCheck extension (indicating that the responder’s certificate was implicitly trusted for its lifetime), and the verification mode was set specifically for OCSP, then the code would simply disable further verification by switching to NO_VERIFY. This approach inadvertently allowed a certificate unrelated to the expected chain to garner an undue degree of trust, as the mere presence of the NoCheck extension would bypass all further trust verification, leading to a serious security vulnerability.

The revised approach rethinks this behavior by distinguishing between different verification contexts. First, it makes sure that the conditional check is only applied in the context of OCSP-specific verification, i.e., when verify equals VERIFY_OCSP. This is an important design decision because it confines the behavior to when an OCSP response is being processed, thereby preventing the NoCheck extension from affecting other types of certificate verification.

Once the context of OCSP verification has been determined, the new logic checks whether the certificate in question carries the NoCheck extension. In the original implementation, the discovery of this extension would directly lead to disabling further certificate validation. Instead, the repair introduces the concept of “downgrading” the verification mode. When the certificate has the NoCheck extension, the verification mode is not set to NO_VERIFY; rather, it is switched to VERIFY_CERT. This downgrade means that the certificate will not bypass verification entirely – it will still be subject to standard certificate chain checks. By doing so, the repair ensures that even though the certificate’s NoCheck extension indicates that the responder’s certificate should be trusted for its lifetime, it will not be improperly validated as if it were directly tied to the chain provided by OCSP.

The intent behind this selective downgrade is to maintain robust security. If a certificate without the NoCheck extension is encountered, the system continues its full OCSP-specific verification routine, ensuring that the certificate is properly tied to the expected chain of trust. Therefore, the existence of the NoCheck extension affects the process only in a very narrowly defined and controlled context. The verification chain is preserved by requiring that OCSP responses, even if they include the NoCheck extension, are still validated via the conventional certificate trust mechanisms. This change prevents a malicious responder from exploiting the NoCheck extension to introduce a fraudulent certificate into the trust chain.

The remedy described by the new logic, therefore, reinforces the proper association between the certificate being validated and the chain of trust expected during OCSP verification. This targeted adjustment prevents the misuse of the NoCheck extension in scenarios where a certificate might otherwise be accepted without proper scrutiny. In summary, the repair ensures that certificates marked with NoCheck continue to be included in the full verification process, albeit via a standard rather than an OCSP-specific verification pathway. This nuanced approach significantly enhances verification security, preventing potential bypass of critical checks, and appropriately limits the influence of the NoCheck extension to the narrow case of OCSP processing without compromising the overall integrity of the chain of trust.
