#### 1. **Understanding the Role of IV in Encryption**

- **Initialization Vector (IV)**: An IV is a random value that is used along with a secret key to encrypt data. It ensures that identical plaintext blocks will produce different ciphertext blocks, even when encrypted with the same key. This randomness is crucial for maintaining the confidentiality and integrity of the encrypted data.
- **Security Implications**: When the IV is not random, patterns in the plaintext can be discerned in the ciphertext, leading to potential vulnerabilities. For example, in AES-CBC mode, if the same key and IV are used to encrypt the same plaintext block, the resulting ciphertext blocks will be identical, revealing information about the plaintext.
