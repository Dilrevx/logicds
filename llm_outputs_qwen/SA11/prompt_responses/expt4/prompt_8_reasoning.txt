### Original Vulnerability

In the original code, the decision to revoke a certificate was based on two conditions: 
1. A match between the certificate's serial number and the serial number of an entry in the Certificate Revocation List (CRL).
2. The `revocationDate` of the entry being in the past.

This dual-check mechanism was intended to ensure that only certificates that were both listed in the CRL and had a revocation date that had already passed would be considered revoked. However, this approach introduced a critical vulnerability. An attacker could manipulate the system's local clock to a date before the `revocationDate`. This manipulation would cause the `Helper.mbedtls_x509_time_is_past(cur.revocationDate)` function to return false, even though the certificate was actually revoked. Consequently, the certificate might be treated as valid, leading to potential security breaches.
