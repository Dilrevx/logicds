### Certificate Management Overview

In Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), certificates are digital documents used to verify the identity of entities in communications over a network. Each certificate is issued by a trusted Certificate Authority (CA) and contains information such as the entity's public key, the issuer's name, the subject's name, the validity period, and a unique serial number. One critical aspect of certificate management is ensuring that certificates are not used after they have been revoked due to compromise or other security issues. This is where Certificate Revocation Lists (CRLs) come into play.

A CRL is a list of certificates that have been revoked but have not yet expired. When a certificate is revoked, its serial number is added to the CRL, and systems using this certificate must check against the CRL to ensure the certificate has not been revoked. The `revocationDate` field in a certificate indicates when the certificate was revoked, but relying solely on this date can be problematic, as demonstrated by the vulnerability in the provided code snippet.
