The vulnerability arises from the fact that the ChaCha20-Poly1305 cipher must always use a unique 12‑byte nonce for each encryption operation. The specification (RFC 7539) mandates a 96‑bit nonce, and any deviation from this (such as using a nonce longer than 12 bytes) may invite dangerous reuse, leading to potential attacks on confidentiality and integrity. In the vulnerable implementation, the function responsible for setting the nonce length would accept values up to a certain constant that inadvertently allowed nonce lengths larger than what is strictly correct for ChaCha20-Poly1305. This opened the door for applications to mistakenly believe that altering the leading bytes of a nonce longer than 12 bytes would create a new, unique value, when in fact the extra bytes were ignored. Such misconfiguration easily leads to nonce reuse, which breaches a fundamental security requirement of this cipher.

The repair addresses this flaw by restricting the maximum allowed nonce length to a constant that properly reflects the limitations imposed by ChaCha20-Poly1305. Instead of relying on a constant used for a different mode or cipher (which could permit lengths that exceed 12 bytes), the updated check compares the supplied nonce length to a maximum specific to ChaCha20-Poly1305. This change ensures that the nonce length cannot be set to a value exceeding what is considered permissible for this cipher. The deliberate selection of a well-defined constant appropriate for ChaCha20-Poly1305 prevents developers from unintentionally using a nonce length that might appear unique due to modifications in the ignored extra bytes, but in reality would be treated the same as a previously used nonce.

In essence, the repair is motivated by the need to enforce strict adherence to the protocol's nonce length requirements. By verifying that the nonce length is within an acceptable range (specifically, no more than the maximum designated for ChaCha20-Poly1305) the code now prevents any deviation that could lead to insecure reuse of nonce values. This careful validation directly mitigates the risk of serious security breaches, such as those resulting from nonce reuse, and ensures that every encryption operation maintains the intended level of cryptographic security.
