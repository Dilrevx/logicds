### Understanding the Vulnerability

In cryptographic protocols, especially those using authenticated encryption with associated data (AEAD) modes, the MAC key plays a crucial role in ensuring the integrity and authenticity of the data. The MAC key should be derived from a secure, unpredictable source, typically a shared secret between the communicating parties, rather than from data that is visible or easily guessable to an attacker, such as the AAD.

The AAD in AEAD modes is used to include additional information in the authentication process without encrypting it. This allows the receiver to verify that the data has not been tampered with and that it originated from the expected sender. However, the AAD itself does not need to be kept secret; it is included in the MAC computation to ensure that any changes to the AAD are detected.
