### Understanding the Vulnerability

In Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), Certificate Revocation Lists (CRLs) play a crucial role in maintaining the security of TLS connections. A CRL is a list of certificates that have been revoked by their issuer before their natural expiration date. During the TLS handshake, a client's certificate must be validated against the CRL to ensure that it has not been revoked. If a certificate is found in the CRL, the connection should be terminated to prevent man-in-the-middle attacks or other security breaches.

The vulnerability in the `tls_verify_crl` function arises from the misuse of the `subject` parameter when retrieving CRLs. Specifically, the function checks the subject field twice instead of checking both the subject and issuer fields. This oversight can lead to a situation where valid CRLs are not recognized, and revoked certificates may pass the validation process, compromising the security of the TLS connection.
