### Role of Initialization Vectors (IVs)

Initialization vectors play a pivotal role in symmetric encryption algorithms, particularly in modes that require them, such as AES-CBC and DES3. An IV is a block of data that is combined with the first block of plaintext before encryption. Its primary function is to ensure that identical plaintext blocks encrypt to different ciphertext blocks, even when using the same key. This property is crucial for maintaining the confidentiality and integrity of the encrypted data.

For modes like AES-CBC and DES3, the IV must be unique and unpredictable for each encryption operation. A non-random IV can lead to several security issues:
- **Pattern Recognition**: Predictable IVs allow attackers to recognize patterns in the ciphertext, potentially revealing information about the plaintext.
- **Replay Attacks**: In some protocols, predictable IVs can facilitate replay attacks, where an attacker can intercept and reuse messages.
- **Known Plaintext Attacks**: With a known plaintext and a predictable IV, an attacker might be able to deduce the encryption key or other sensitive information.
