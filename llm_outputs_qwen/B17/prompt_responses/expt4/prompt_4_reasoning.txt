#### Role of Issuer vs. Subject in CRL Validation

- **Issuer**: The issuer of a certificate is the CA that signed the certificate. When validating a certificate, it is crucial to check the CRL issued by this CA to see if the certificate has been revoked.
- **Subject**: The subject of a certificate is the entity to which the certificate was issued (e.g., the client or server). The subject's CRL does not exist; CRLs are always issued by the issuer (CA) and contain information about certificates that the issuer has revoked.

### Impact of the Misuse

Using the `subject` parameter instead of the `issuer` results in the following issues:
- **Incorrect CRL Retrieval**: The system attempts to retrieve a CRL based on the client's certificate rather than the CA's certificate. Since no such CRL exists, the validation process might fail to identify revoked certificates correctly.
- **Security Risk**: Clients whose certificates have been revoked by the CA could potentially establish a connection to the server, bypassing the intended security measures. This can lead to man-in-the-middle attacks or unauthorized access.
