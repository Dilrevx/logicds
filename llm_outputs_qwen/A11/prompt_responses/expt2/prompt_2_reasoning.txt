### Ensuring Accurate System Time

The primary vulnerability in the original code lies in its reliance on the local system's time for determining whether a certificate has been revoked. An attacker can exploit this by manipulating the system's clock, leading to incorrect conclusions about the validity of a certificate. To mitigate this risk, the system must ensure that its time is synchronized with a reliable external time reference, such as a Network Time Protocol (NTP) server. This synchronization process helps in maintaining an accurate system time, which is crucial for proper validation of certificates and their revocation dates.

### Supplementary Checks for Robust Validation

In addition to accurate time synchronization, the repair introduces several supplementary checks to ensure the integrity and authenticity of the Certificate Revocation List (CRL):

1. **CRL Signature Verification**: The function `verify_crl_signature(cur)` is called to check the digital signature of the CRL. A valid CRL must have a correct signature from a trusted Certificate Authority (CA). This verification step ensures that the CRL has not been tampered with and is indeed issued by a legitimate CA.

2. **Issuer Trust Check**: The function `is_issuer_trusted(cur)` verifies that the issuer of the CRL is trusted. This step is essential because only CRLs issued by trusted CAs are considered authoritative. By checking the trustworthiness of the issuer, the system can avoid accepting malicious or compromised CRLs.

3. **CRL Validity Period Check**: The function `is_crl_valid(cur)` ensures that the CRL itself is within its validity period. A CRL that has expired or is not yet valid should not be used to make decisions about certificate revocation. This check prevents the use of outdated or prematurely issued CRLs.
